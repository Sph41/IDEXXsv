# Competitive Matrix: Streak & Habit Micro-Learning Apps

*Scope: apps built around streaks, daily habits, and micro-learning. Excludes social platforms and long-form course platforms. Researched September 2026.*

## Competitors

### Duolingo

- **Core features:** Gamified language lessons, XP, leagues/leaderboards, hearts/energy system, stories, AI conversation practice.
- **Pricing:** Free (ad-supported); Super Duolingo ~$6.99–12.99/mo depending on term; Family Plan ~$119.99/yr for up to 6 accounts.
- **Target customer:** Broad global consumer base, casual-to-serious learners who respond to gamification and competition.
- **Post-week-1 engagement:** Streak count is the central mechanic, reinforced daily with goal-completion celebration animations. Streak Freeze can be bought with Gems, but Gems are also earned for free over time — it isn't a pure paywall. Recovery also has a social/emotional layer: Duolingo itself (not the user) can prompt a friend to gift Gems to repair someone's broken streak, pulling a third person into the emotional stakes of the loss. A newer "Streak Repair" (part of Super) auto-restores a lost streak under certain conditions. Reminder notifications (the "Duo" mascot) are widely known for a guilt-driven tone. Leagues/leaderboards also pull users back.
- **Notable recent changes:** A 2025 internal "AI-first" memo caused major public backlash into 2026, with users publicly abandoning multi-year streaks (some 1,000+ days) in protest; CEO walked the policy back in May 2026. Notably, this shows how emotionally load-bearing the streak mechanic is — it became the thing users sacrificed to make a point.

### Babbel

- **Core features:** Structured, CEFR-aligned courses by native language, speech recognition, podcasts, live classes, and a new AI pronunciation-feedback beta ("Babbel Speak").
- **Pricing:** ~$8–17/mo depending on subscription length (3/6/12-month terms); lifetime plan around $299.
- **Target customer:** Adults with a practical goal (travel, work) — more curriculum-driven than gamification-driven.
- **Post-week-1 engagement:** Tracks streaks and badges, but deliberately less gamified than Duolingo — no leagues, no achievement rewards beyond streaks/certificates. Relies on spaced-repetition review prompts rather than competitive or social mechanics.
- **Notable recent changes:** Rollout of "Babbel Speak," an AI pronunciation-feedback feature trained on diverse accents.

### Elevate

- **Core features:** 40+ short games across reading, writing, speaking, math, and memory; a personalized daily workout; a study-tips section; weekly progress reports.
- **Pricing:** Free tier (3 games/day, no skill selection); Pro ~$9.99/mo or ~$39.99/yr.
- **Target customer:** Adults/professionals seeking general cognitive sharpening — closest to Streakly's own "5 minutes a day" positioning, but for brain-training rather than a named skill track.
- **Post-week-1 engagement:** Daily streak count, 150+ unlockable achievements, and a workout calendar for visual progress. No streak-freeze, repair, or comeback mechanic was found in available research — engagement appears to rely on achievement unlocks and progress visualization rather than any forgiveness mechanic.
- **Notable recent changes:** No significant recent product changes surfaced in research.

### Memrise

- **Core features:** Native-speaker video lessons, an AI conversation bot, grammar lessons, structured courses, offline downloads.
- **Pricing:** Pro subscription, roughly $8–25/mo or ~$60–62/yr depending on region/promotion; lifetime plans (heavily discounted) around $99–330.
- **Target customer:** Language learners who want authentic, native-speaker content and immersion, overlapping with the Duolingo/Babbel audience.
- **Post-week-1 engagement:** Streaks plus a "growing plant" gamification metaphor and reminder notifications.
- **Notable recent changes:** Discontinued its original user-generated flashcard courses (fully removed by March 2024), pivoting entirely to professionally produced native-speaker video content. Memrise reported average session duration roughly doubled (7 to 14 minutes) after the pivot — a signal that content quality drove deeper engagement more than community-generated breadth did.

### Brilliant

- **Core features:** Interactive, hands-on STEM lessons (math, CS, science, data); a new real-time AI tutor, "Koji," that coaches users through problems live using voice (built with ElevenLabs); 40+ courses.
- **Pricing:** Premium roughly $20/mo billed annually (~$240/yr) or ~$30 month-to-month; Family plan ~$40/mo for 6 seats. (Sources vary somewhat on exact figures.)
- **Target customer:** STEM-curious adults and students wanting rigorous, skill-specific learning — arguably the closest analog to Streakly's "learn a skill in 5 minutes a day" pitch, minus the streak-centric framing.
- **Post-week-1 engagement:** No evidence found of a streak-freeze or comeback mechanic in available research. The primary retention lever appears to be the Koji AI tutor and course progression rather than a streak system specifically.
- **Notable recent changes:** Launched Koji, a real-time voice AI tutor, in May 2026 — free users get a limited preview, Premium gets full access. Signals a strategic bet on AI-guided personalization as the retention driver, not gamification.

## White Space for Streakly

**1. Duolingo makes streak recovery emotional and social, but nobody designs the return moment itself as an acknowledgment.** Duolingo's recovery mechanic isn't purely transactional — Gems can be earned for free (not just bought), the app itself can prompt a friend to gift Gems to repair someone's streak, and daily goals are celebrated with animation. That's real emotional and social scaffolding. But all of it is about *preventing or undoing* the loss (via currency, social pressure, or an automated repair) — none of it is about how the app greets the user in the moment they actually come back after a real gap. Babbel, Elevate, Memrise, and Brilliant have no recovery mechanic at all; a broken streak is simply a reset. This lines up with what Streakly's own users are asking for (per [nps-analysis.md](nps-analysis.md)): a "coach," not a "scorekeeper." The gap isn't "make it emotional" — Duolingo already did that — it's "design the acknowledgment of the return itself," which no one in this set appears to do.

**2. Nobody addresses streak anxiety before a break happens.** Every retention mechanic observed across all five competitors — freezes, repairs, achievements, plant metaphors, reminders — activates reactively, after a miss is imminent or has already occurred. None of them appear to address the anticipatory dread a new user feels in week 1, before they've ever broken a streak (the exact pattern seen in [interview-synthesis.md](interview-synthesis.md) with Amara). This is open ground: a comeback-focused mechanic alone would still leave that pre-break anxiety unaddressed, just as it's currently unaddressed everywhere else in the category.
