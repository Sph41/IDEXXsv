# Change Log

## Day 1 — 2026-09-28 — Discovery

- Phase: Discovery kicked off for the Streakly Day-7 retention drop (48% → 39% since the streak redesign).
- Populated [project.md](project.md) with product overview, squad, current phase, and key stakeholders.
- Drafted [strategy.md](strategy.md): working hypothesis is that the punishing streak-break experience (hard reset, no acknowledgment, harsh notification) is driving the drop — not yet validated as root cause.
- Open decision: confirm root cause before designing any solution (targeted for Thursday squad meeting).
- Built first skill: [weekly-status](../skills/weekly-status.md) — formats raw notes into a Shipped/In Progress/Blockers/Next Week leadership update. Linked from [orientation.md](orientation.md).
- Synthesized 3 user interviews into [02-research/interview-synthesis.md](../02-research/interview-synthesis.md). Key finding: the week-1 user interviewed hadn't broken a streak yet, but was already anxious about it — anticipatory loss-anxiety, not just post-break failure, appears to hurt week-1 retention. This complicates the working hypothesis in strategy.md, which currently frames the fix as a post-break "comeback" experience; that wouldn't address users who churn from dread before ever breaking a streak.
- Analyzed 10 raw NPS comments into [02-research/nps-analysis.md](../02-research/nps-analysis.md), prepared for Marcus. Top theme (6 of 10 comments): streak resets with no recovery path, directly tied to reported uninstalls. Also surfaced two distinct secondary issues: punitive/untuned notifications, and no state-aware acknowledgment on the home screen. One user named a competitor's streak-freeze feature directly, corroborating Lena's early Comeback screen concept.
- Researched 5 competitors (Duolingo, Babbel, Elevate, Memrise, Brilliant) into [02-research/competitive-matrix.md](../02-research/competitive-matrix.md). White space for Streakly: (1) even Duolingo's emotional/social streak-repair mechanics (earned Gems, app-initiated friend gifting, daily celebrations) are about preventing/undoing loss, not acknowledging the return moment itself — nobody designs the comeback as an experience; (2) all competitor mechanics are reactive to an actual or imminent miss, none address pre-break anxiety in new users (matches Amara's week-1 dread in interview-synthesis.md).
